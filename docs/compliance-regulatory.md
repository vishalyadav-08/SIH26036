## 1. Compliance Overview
Compliance is not merely a bureaucratic checkbox for a national platform like MapanSetu; it is the foundational pillar of trust, security, and operational viability. Handling highly sensitive geospatial data, property records, and personal identifying information (PII) of millions of citizens necessitates stringent adherence to multiple layers of regulations. 

MapanSetu is engineered from the ground up with a "Compliance-by-Design" philosophy. This ensures that:
- **Citizen Trust is Maintained:** Protecting the data privacy and rights of the citizens.
- **Legal Ramifications are Mitigated:** Preventing legal liabilities associated with data breaches or non-compliance.
- **Interoperability is Guaranteed:** Conforming to national and international standards ensures smooth integration with other government systems.
- **National Security is Preserved:** Strict adherence to geospatial data policies prevents the exposure of critical defense and strategic assets.

This document outlines the comprehensive compliance posture of MapanSetu, mapping our technical and operational strategies to relevant legal, regulatory, and technical standards.

## 2. Indian Legal Framework Compliance

### 2.1. Information Technology Act, 2000 (IT Act)
The IT Act forms the bedrock of digital governance in India. MapanSetu complies with all relevant sections:
*   **Section 43A (Data Protection):** MapanSetu implements "reasonable security practices and procedures" to protect sensitive personal data or information (SPDI). We use AES-256 encryption and robust access controls to prevent unauthorized access, damage, or data extraction.
*   **Section 65B (Electronic Records as Evidence):** All electronic maps, approvals, and transaction logs generated by MapanSetu are digitally signed, timestamped, and stored immutably to ensure they are legally admissible as evidence in Indian courts. Our architecture ensures non-repudiation.
*   **Section 72A (Breach of Confidentiality):** Strict NDAs and Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) ensure that no official or vendor can disclose citizen data without explicit consent or legal mandate. Violations are tracked and automatically reported.

### 2.2. Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDPA)
As a Data Fiduciary processing personal data of Indian citizens, MapanSetu strictly adheres to the DPDPA 2023:
*   **Consent Management:** A dynamic consent architecture allows users to give, review, and withdraw consent for data processing (e.g., fetching KYC details via Aadhaar/DigiLocker). Consent logs are immutably stored.
*   **Data Principal Rights:** Users have comprehensive dashboards to exercise their rights to access, correction, and erasure (where not conflicting with property laws).
*   **Data Fiduciary Obligations:** We implement data minimization, processing only what is necessary for map approvals and record management.
*   **Cross-border Data Transfer Restrictions:** 100% of MapanSetu's data infrastructure, including primary, secondary, and disaster recovery sites, is hosted within India (Data Sovereignty). No data leaves Indian geographical boundaries.
*   **Data Retention Policies:** PII is retained only as long as necessary for the purpose, subject to overarching archival mandates for land records.
*   **Grievance Redressal Mechanism:** A dedicated portal with SLA-bound ticketing for users to raise data privacy concerns, overseen by an appointed Data Protection Officer (DPO).

### 2.3. Right to Information Act, 2005 (RTI)
Transparency in public operations is enforced via RTI integration:
*   **Proactive Disclosure Requirements:** General statistics, platform performance metrics, SOPs, and anonymized approval rates are proactively published on the platform under Section 4(1)(b) of the RTI Act.
*   **Information Request Handling:** Integration with the central RTI portal to swiftly route requests related to specific map approvals or land records to the designated Public Information Officer (PIO).

### 2.4. Indian Evidence Act
To ensure electronic records (digitally signed maps and property mutations) hold up in court, MapanSetu uses PKI (Public Key Infrastructure) digital signatures, strong hashing algorithms, and secure timestamping authorities to prove the integrity and origin of all digital artifacts.

### 2.5. Registration Act, 1908
MapanSetu bridges the gap between spatial data and property registration. Workflows ensure that any division or merger of land parcels visually represented on the map is synchronized with the sub-registrar's digital records, preventing double-registration or registration of unapproved layouts.

### 2.6. Transfer of Property Act, 1882
The system validates that the transferor has a clear, unencumbered title (via integration with the encumbrance certificate database) before allowing a spatial mutation (subdivision) to proceed on the map.

### 2.7. Indian Stamp Act, 1899
Through integration with the e-Stamping systems (like SHCIL) and state treasuries, MapanSetu calculates the requisite stamp duty for property transactions arising from map approvals (e.g., layout approvals) and logs the payment reference immutably.

### 2.8. Geospatial Guidelines 2021 (Ministry of Science & Technology)
Compliance with the liberalization of geospatial data is critical:
*   **What data can be publicly shared:** Non-sensitive geographic data is made openly accessible via APIs to promote the domestic geospatial ecosystem.
*   **Resolution Restrictions:** Adherence to spatial accuracy thresholds (e.g., 1m horizontal accuracy restrictions where applicable for specific non-government entities) when exposing data to private parties.
*   **Defense/Strategic Area Restrictions:** Negative list areas (military installations, critical infrastructure, border areas) are automatically masked or heavily degraded in public views. Only authorized defense and intelligence personnel have access to unmasked data in these zones via a secure, air-gapped terminal approach or highly restricted VPNs.

## 4. International Standards

### 4.1. ISO 27001 - Information Security Management
The entire software development lifecycle (SDLC) and operational procedures of MapanSetu are mapped to ISO 27001 controls. This includes risk assessment, asset management, access control, cryptography, and incident management policies.

### 4.2. ISO 27018 - PII Protection in Cloud
Specific controls are implemented for protecting Personally Identifiable Information (PII) in our cloud infrastructure, aligning with ISO 27018, ensuring transparency on where data is stored and who has access to it.

### 4.3. ISO 19115 - Geographic Information Metadata
All spatial datasets managed within MapanSetu are cataloged using ISO 19115 metadata standards, ensuring high discoverability and standardized descriptions of data lineage, quality, and spatial reference systems.

### 4.4. ISO 19139 - Geographic Information XML Schema
The implementation of the ISO 19115 metadata standard using the ISO 19139 XML schema for robust, machine-readable cataloging and data exchange.

### 4.5. OGC Standards (Open Geospatial Consortium)
To ensure MapanSetu is not a data silo, it fully supports OGC open standards for interoperability:
*   **WMS (Web Map Service):** Serving rendered map images to client applications (e.g., state portals).
*   **WFS (Web Feature Service):** Providing transactional access (read/write/update) to underlying vector features (land parcels).
*   **WCS (Web Coverage Service):** For sharing raster data like satellite imagery and DEMs.
*   **GML (Geography Markup Language):** Used as the primary data exchange format for spatial features.

### 4.6. INSPIRE Directive Alignment
While European, the INSPIRE (Infrastructure for Spatial Information in Europe) directive represents the gold standard for spatial data infrastructure. MapanSetu incorporates its principles regarding data harmonization, network services, and spatial data themes to ensure global interoperability.

## 5. Security Compliance

### 5.1. CERT-In Guidelines
*   **Incident Reporting:** MapanSetu's SOC (Security Operations Center) is configured to detect, log, and report any cybersecurity incidents (breaches, DDoS, malware) to CERT-In within the mandated **6-hour window**.
*   **Log Retention:** All application, network, and security logs are retained securely for a minimum of **180 days** on nearline storage, and archived for 5 years for forensic analysis.
*   **KYC for Users:** Strict identity verification via Aadhaar/PAN integration for all land owners, architects, and government officials interacting with the system.

### 5.2. NCIIPC Guidelines
Given the platform handles critical land and geospatial records, MapanSetu is designed anticipating classification as Critical Information Infrastructure (CII). 
*   Physical and logical separation of critical networks.
*   Air-gapped backups.
*   Strict personnel vetting processes.

### 5.3. Data Encryption Standards
*   **At Rest:** All databases, file systems storing documents, and backups are encrypted using **AES-256-GCM**.
*   **In Transit:** All communication between clients (browsers/mobile apps) and servers, and between microservices, is secured using **TLS 1.3** with strong cipher suites.
*   **Key Management:** Cryptographic keys are generated, stored, and rotated using a FIPS 140-2 Level 3 compliant Hardware Security Module (HSM) or equivalent Cloud KMS.

### 5.4. Access Control
*   **RBAC Implementation:** Granular Role-Based Access Control mapping organizational hierarchies (e.g., Junior Engineer -> Assistant Town Planner -> District Collector).
*   **Principle of Least Privilege:** Users are granted only the minimum permissions necessary to perform their duties.
*   **Session Management:** Secure, HttpOnly, SameSite cookies; aggressive session timeouts; concurrent login restrictions; and forced re-authentication for sensitive actions (like final map approval).

### 5.5. Audit Requirements
*   **Complete Audit Trails:** Every CRUD (Create, Read, Update, Delete) operation on map data, user profiles, or permissions creates an immutable audit log capturing *Who*, *What*, *When*, *Where* (IP), and *Why*.
*   **Tamper-proof Logging:** Logs are written to append-only storage and cryptographically hashed periodically to detect tampering.
*   **Regular Audits:** Scheduled quarterly internal audits of access logs and system configurations.

### 5.6. Penetration Testing
*   **Annual VAPT:** Vulnerability Assessment and Penetration Testing will be conducted annually (and after major releases) by a CERT-In empaneled auditor.
*   **Remediation Timelines:** Critical vulnerabilities must be patched within 48 hours; high within 7 days; medium within 30 days.

## 6. Data Governance

*   **Data Classification Policy:** Data is classified into Public, Internal, Confidential, and Restricted tiers. Controls are applied based on classification.
*   **Data Retention Schedule:** Transactional records are kept live for 7 years; historical spatial mutations are kept perpetually in cold storage for title lineage tracking.
*   **Data Backup and Archival:** Automated daily incremental and weekly full backups. Archival data is stored in geo-redundant storage across multiple seismic zones in India.
*   **Data Anonymization for Analytics:** When generating reports on land use patterns or urbanization trends, PII of landowners is masked or stripped to preserve privacy.
*   **Data Sovereignty:** Hard constraint: No data processing or storage occurs outside the sovereign territory of the Republic of India.
*   **Right to Erasure Implementation:** Automated workflows to safely delete user accounts and associated non-statutory data upon request, ensuring referential integrity of core land records is maintained.
